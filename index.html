01 · About

Close to the hardware

I'm an embedded firmware engineer and an MS student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Northeastern University, concentrating in Computer Systems and Software. Most of my work lives where code meets circuits: C and Python on microcontrollers, sensors on I²C, SPI and UART, and debugging with JTAG, oscilloscopes and logic analyzers.

Before Boston, I studied Electronics Engineering at Shri Ramdeobaba College of Engineering and Management in Nagpur. In a four-person research team I wrote the STM32 firmware for a solar-powered UV-tracking wearable that became a Springer publication. At MaprGo I built IoT devices whose firmware improved sensor accuracy by 25% and energy efficiency by 30%. I'm also a named inventor on two patents.

At Northeastern I've pushed CUDA kernels to a 920× speedup, published an open-source hardware-testing library on PyPI, and I now mentor undergraduates as a graduate TA in the embedded systems lab.
